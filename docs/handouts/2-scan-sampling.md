# Lab 2 · Scan sampling: how many are feeding right now?

**Time:** 25–35 min · **Setup:** Vivarium, Brains or Mixed A/B mode. Use the class link if your instructor gives one.

## Goal
Practise **scan sampling**: counting how many animals in a group are doing each behaviour at one instant. Then separate two sources of error:
- **counting error:** you miscounted what was in front of you;
- **placement error:** where you looked wasn't representative of the whole population.

## Steps
1. **Field** tab → Method **Scan sample**. Pick a species that lives in groups (capybara works well), and choose 20 scans every 15 sim-s.
2. At each scan the range pauses with a **dashed circle**. Every animal of your species inside it shows its posture cue.
3. Type how many are **head down (feeding)** and how many are **head up**, then press Record. Enter moves between the boxes. Press **Lost count** if you couldn't count. Leave fleeing animals out.
4. Read your results and download your data.

## Questions
1. Compare columns 1 and 2. Is your counting error random, or do you consistently over- or under-count one posture?
2. Compare columns 2 and 3. The circles are always centred on an animal. Why does that make them land in groups, and why does it bias the estimate?
3. Propose a way to place scan areas without that bias.
4. Your class pooled its data. Does pooling fix counting error? Does it fix placement error?

## Instructor notes
- **Q1:** Head-up animals at the edge of a dense group are the usual misses. Students often see a bias towards counting "feeding", because the head-down cue is more compact.
- **Q2:** Centring on an animal oversamples dense groups and misses loners. In testing, circles showed capybaras 90% feeding against 86% for the whole population. If loners behave differently (they often scan more), every circle is biased in the same direction.
- **Q3:** Random points or a fixed grid, accepting that some scans are empty.
- **Q4:** Pooling averages away *random* counting error, but not *placement* error: a systematic bias stays in every student's data. This is the key lesson.
